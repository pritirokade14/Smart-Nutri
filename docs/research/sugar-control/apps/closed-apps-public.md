# Closed apps: public research for Sugar Control (Phase 2)

Date: 2026-11-07. Public pages only (store listings, help or marketing pages, news, guideline summaries). No downloads, decompiling or logins. Everything is paraphrased. Items marked [unverified] come from a single secondary source, an aggregator, or could not be confirmed. Several official pages blocked automated fetches (403 or 404): healthifyme.com diabetes pages, mobihealthnews.com, diabetes.org plate pages, cdc.gov, nin.res.in dietary-guidelines PDF, professional.diabetes.org PDF, sugar.fit root, bluecircle.foundation blog (expired certificate). Where blocked, only search-result summaries were available and this is noted.

Respects `SHORTLIST.md` do-not-build list: no insulin or dose calculators, no estimated HbA1c, no alarms or treatment targets, no diagnosis or risk scoring, no CGM or pump automation. Ideas below are filtered through that list; mySugr's bolus calculator and estimated HbA1c are recorded only as things NOT to copy.

## 1. Per-app findings

### sugar.fit (India, paid, coach-led)
- Onboarding: not described publicly. The App Store text says a physical examination is needed to get a subscription, so it looks clinic-gated. https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id1568271882
- Glucose logging and CGM: the programme is built on a CGM that gives real-time feedback on how food, exercise and habits move glucose. A hardware bundle (four 15-day sensors with a free 1:1 coach and app) was listed by a retailer at about Rs 13,499 [unverified: third-party price tracker]. https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id1568271882 , https://www.pricebefore.com/sugar-fit-smart-glucose-monitoring-sensor-p970507.html
- Meal-glucose link: described as CGM showing what drives spikes; no screen-level detail public [unverified]. Store listing above.
- Carb budgets, GI/GL, plate method: nothing public found.
- Indian food and plans: doctors and dietitians build a customised diet plan; coaches give personalised nutrition plans (user review). https://apps.apple.com/in/app/sugar-fit/id1568271882
- HbA1c entry, reports: nothing public found.
- PCOS: no sugar.fit PCOS programme found (the PCOS plans in results were other sellers). Search result summary, no single URL.
- Coaching vs self-serve: heavily coach-led (diabetes and nutrition experts, session booking); also mentions GLP-1 medicine support. https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id1568271882
- Free vs paid: app is free to download, premium needs a subscription, no price shown on the listing. Same URL.
- Complaints: CGM never delivered or delayed, poor support, "scam" claim, too many sales calls (App Store reviews); unwanted marketing messages and near-expiry stock (consumercomplaints.in). Trustpilot reviews are mostly 4 to 5 stars praising coaches. https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id1568271882 , https://www.consumercomplaints.in/bycompany/sugar-fit-a621741.html , https://uk.trustpilot.com/review/www.sugarfit.com
- Idea worth borrowing (information-only): a plain "your plate vs spike" explanation from the user's own logged meals, written as general information. Coaching and CGM hardware are out of scope.

### BeatO (India, hardware-led)
- Onboarding: not publicly described.
- Logging: readings from the BeatO glucometer sync automatically into a diary, with simple graphs and colour-coded results; works with the BeatO Curv meter. https://apps.apple.com/in/app/beato/id1070739458
- Meal tags, units, quick entry: not stated [unverified].
- Meal-glucose link: CGM bundle pitches AI glucose and diet insights [unverified, marketing via aggregator]. https://www.pricebefore.com/beato-cgm-continuous-glucose-monitor-device-p1146184.html
- Carb budgets, GI/GL, plate: none found. Diet plan comes from nutritionists/coaches (listing text).
- HbA1c: the app lets users monitor HbA1c and get reminders (user-entered tracking). https://apps.apple.com/in/app/beato/id1070739458 . An "estimated HbA1c" is mentioned only for the CGM bundle: do not copy.
- Reports: results can be emailed in a readable format with trend graphs. Same listing.
- PCOS: none found.
- Coaching: doctor consults and nutritionist plans, sold with devices.
- Free vs paid: app free; strips, meter and bundles are the revenue. TrustRadius lists no free trial or freemium [unverified]. https://www.trustradius.com/compare-products/beato-diabetes-management-app
- Complaints: meter connection failures, a log-timestamp bug (2022), unwanted calls from executives after readings were shared (2021), an undelivered meter despite paid strips. https://apps.apple.com/in/app/beato/id1070739458 , BeatO TrustRadius page above.
- Idea worth borrowing: colour-coded but non-alarming reading history with one-tap email share of user-entered data.

### mySugr (Roche, global)
- Onboarding: not covered by sources fetched [unverified].
- Logging: meals, carbs, medication, glucose and insulin entries; auto-logging from supported meters, Apple Health and Google Fit. Reviewers call data entry inefficient and say day-by-day scrolling is tedious. https://apps.apple.com/au/app/id516509211
- Tags: free app has basic tags; Pro adds more tags and a searchable store of places, meals, activities. Same URL; https://www.mysugr.com/blog/3-benefits-of-mysugr-pro
- Meal-glucose link: Pro meal photos build a personal food database, and the app shows how glucose responded so the user can judge their carb estimate. https://www.mysugr.com/blog/3-benefits-of-mysugr-pro
- Carbs: carb intake is logged; no per-meal budget feature found.
- GI/GL, Indian food, plate method: none found.
- HbA1c: shows an "estimated HbA1c" (an estimate: SmartNutri must not do this). https://apps.apple.com/au/app/id516509211 . It is unclear whether lab values can be entered [unverified].
- Reports: daily, weekly and monthly reports to share; Pro adds PDF and Excel export. https://apps.apple.com/au/app/id516509211
- PCOS: none.
- Coaching vs self-serve: self-serve, with gamified "monster" logging [unverified, from general knowledge, not fetched].
- Free vs paid: free with in-app purchases. Pro listed at 6.99 USD per month or 59.99 USD per year on one storefront; other regions differ. Pro is free with some Accu-Chek meters if synced at least every 30 days. https://apps.apple.com/au/app/id516509211 , https://accu-chek.co.uk/mysugr-app
- Bolus calculator exists in some countries: regulated device feature, do not mirror.
- Idea worth borrowing: meal photo plus later glucose note on the same entry; "how close was my estimate" framing for carbs.

### Glucose Buddy (Azumio)
- Logging: glucose, medication and meals in one entry, with notes; mg/dL and mmol/L supported. https://apps.apple.com/in/app/id533299240
- Carbs: carb intake through a food database, with hourly view of glucose and carbs. Same URL.
- Meal tags: custom tags reported as premium [unverified, aggregator summary]. https://apps.apple.com/app/id294754639
- A1C: trends for glucose, insulin, weight and A1C; an automatic A1C calculator is described as premium (an estimate, do not copy). Same URLs.
- Reports: printable PDF and CSV for a clinician. https://apps.apple.com/in/app/id533299240
- Integrations: Apple Health, Dexcom [unverified], steps and cardio auto-tracked.
- GI/GL, Indian food, PCOS, coaching: none found; self-serve.
- Free vs paid: India listing shows the app at Rs 199 with premium options Rs 769 to Rs 4,599 (durations unstated). Same URL.
- Complaints: only an old (2013) freeze-on-log review on that page; a reviewer elsewhere objected to automatic recurring charges [unverified].
- Idea worth borrowing: dual-unit support and a single-entry form for glucose plus meal note.

### HealthifyMe (India; diabetes via HealthifyPro / BIOS)
- Onboarding: not publicly detailed (healthifyme.com diabetes pages returned 403).
- CGM and meal response: combines CGM patterns with food and step data to suggest foods and nudges; the AI coach "Ria" is said to use glucose data. https://biospectrumindia.com/news/98/21095/healthifyme-launches-healthifypro-new-technology-in-metabolic-health.html , https://nutriscan.app/blog/posts/healthifyme-cgm-plan-cost-food-glucose-support-45028da9fd (third-party blog) [unverified accuracy]
- Programme content: BIOS bundle with CGM, smart scale, a broad metabolic panel and human coaches; diabetes page mentions two coaches, a doctor and group workouts (search summary). Same BiospectrumIndia URL.
- Indian food: database claimed to cover 100,000+ foods including regional dishes [unverified]. https://nutriscan.app/blog/posts/healthifyme-pricing-2026-india-plans-63a87b21d0
- Carb budgets, GI/GL, plate, HbA1c, reports, PCOS: no public detail found; no PCOS-specific plan found (PCOS appears only as blog content).
- Coaching: strong human plus AI coaching.
- Price (third-party estimates only): Smart CGM Plan about Rs 4,099 one-time; Pro plans with CGM Rs 5,500 to Rs 8,000 per month [unverified]. Same NutriScan URL.
- Complaints: not gathered (no reliable source reached).
- Idea worth borrowing: the "food ranked by how your body responded" view, but only from user-typed meal and glucose notes, not CGM.

### Blue Circle Diabetes (India nonprofit, free)
- Features: glucose log, carb-counting support, food lookup with GI and GL, portion guidance from a "peer-reviewed food database", an "Ask Nupur" tool estimating how spiky a food may be, a moderated peer community, and an insulin calculator (do not copy). Covers type 1, type 2, LADA, MODY and gestational diabetes. https://www.bluecircle.foundation/projects/blue-circle-diabetes-mobile-app
- Units: mmol/L was added after user demand. https://blog.bluecircle.foundation/blue-circle-diabetes-mobile-app/ (via search snippet; page itself not fetchable)
- Food web tool: shows carbs, protein, fat, fibre, GI and "more" for any searched food; GL, Indian coverage and data sources are not stated. https://www.bluecircle.foundation/food
- Free: free, no ads, open to all. Same project page.
- Coaching: peer support (buddy calls), not clinical coaching.
- Onboarding, HbA1c, reports, PCOS, complaints: not found [unverified]. Languages not stated.
- Idea worth borrowing: GI plus GL plus portion on one food card, with a plain "spiky or steady" label drawn from labelled data (no medical claim).

## 2. Cross-app patterns for SmartNutri (information side only)
1. Meal note beside a glucose reading (mySugr, Glucose Buddy): simple, safe.
2. Doctor share of user-entered data (BeatO email, mySugr and Glucose Buddy PDF/CSV).
3. GI/GL and portion on a food card (Blue Circle).
4. Coach-led spike explanation is the paid moat of sugar.fit and HealthifyMe; self-serve apps do not offer it.
5. Gaps nobody documents publicly: gentle condition onboarding, per-meal carb budget from a packaged-food label, PCOS-specific tracking (only separate Indian PCOS programmes exist: GoodFlip, Fitterfly, per search results [unverified]).
6. Common complaints to avoid: hardware delivery problems, constant sales calls, slow data entry, surprise recurring charges, timestamp bugs.

## 3. Citable guidance (all PENDING CLINICIAN REVIEW; general information, never alarms or treatment targets)

Source quality note: only secondary summaries were reachable for several items. A clinician or the primary document must confirm each before it goes into `apps/api/app/scoring/data/*.yaml`.

- **Glycaemic index bands:** low 55 or less, medium 56 to 69, high 70 or more. Seen consistently in secondary sources (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glycemic_index , Singapore KTPH poster https://www.ktph.com.sg/healthlibrary/Documents/Nutr%20Posters/KTPH_[Colour]%20Glycaemic%20Index%20of%20Common%20Foods%20(E).pdf). The Wikipedia page cites a GI Diet chart rather than a primary paper. Primary candidate: Atkinson et al., International Tables of GI and GL, Diabetes Care 2008 (bands not confirmed in the abstract) [unverified]; a 2021 edition exists in Am J Clin Nutr 114(5):1625-1632 [unverified]. GL bands from a secondary summary: low 10 or less, medium 11 to 19, high 20 or more [unverified].
- **ICMR-NIN (India):** Dietary Guidelines for Indians 2024 were released 7 May 2024, with 17 guidelines and a GI annexure (Annexure III). https://www.nin.res.in/dietaryguidelines/pdfjs/locale/DGI24thJune2024fin.pdf (blocked this session; facts from search summary). The "My Plate for the Day" sheet puts vegetables, fruit, leafy greens and roots at about half of the day's foods, with cereals about 40 to 44 percent of energy on a 2,000 kcal diet; gram amounts differ by edition (2023 sheet: cereals 260 g, pulses 85 g, vegetables 400 g, fruit 100 g). https://nin.res.in/downloads/My%20plate%20for%20the%20day-english-09-10-2023.pdf [unverified numbers]
- **ICMR-INDIAB diet study:** reported carbohydrate 49 to 54 percent of energy for diabetes remission and 54 to 57 percent for preventing progression from prediabetes; write-ups vary slightly. Reported by news summaries only: https://deccanchronicle.com/nation/current-affairs/270822/dietary-changes-can-lead-to-diabetes-remission-icmr-study.html [unverified]. Do not present as a per-meal figure.
- **RSSDI:** a review says RSSDI allows up to 45 to 65 percent of energy from carbohydrate, mostly low-GI sources, protein up to 15 percent. https://www.springermedizin.de/insights-on-medical-nutrition-therapy-for-type-2-diabetes-mellit/16454482 [unverified: secondary]. No published RSSDI or ICMR "grams of carbohydrate per meal" figure was found. Finding: do not ship a per-meal carb number from India sources; treat the budget as user or clinician set.
- **International macronutrient range (for context):** Diabetes Canada 2018, carbohydrate 45 to 60 percent of energy, individualised. ADA says there is no single ideal split. Search summary, no URL fetched [unverified].
- **Plate method:** the ADA Diabetes Plate Method uses a 9-inch plate with non-starchy vegetables taking the largest share, then protein and carbohydrate foods in the remaining parts; starchy vegetables, grains and (per some sources) beans count as carbohydrate. Secondary pages only: https://www.canr.msu.edu/news/create_your_plate_for_diabetes . Exact quarter proportions need the primary ADA page (diabetes.org 404 this session). No IDF plate source was found, so do not attribute the plate method to IDF [unverified].
- **Glucose reference ranges (info only):** ADA Standards of Care glycaemic goals for many non-pregnant adults, as summarised: A1C below 7 percent (looser, such as below 8 percent, for some people); before-meal capillary glucose 80 to 130 mg/dL; peak 1 to 2 hours after a meal under 180 mg/dL; for CGM users, over 70 percent of time between 70 and 180 mg/dL and under 4 percent below 70. https://www.linksmedicus.com/news/comprehensive-glycemic-goals-and-hypoglycemia-management-in-diabetes-2025-ada-standards/ (secondary, 2025 edition). The 2026 Standards loosen goals for older adults, so recheck [unverified]. Primary: https://diabetesjournals.org/care (Standards of Care, Section 6; page itself returned 403). Product rule: show only as "general reference, ask your clinician", never as a target, alert or colour-coded verdict; user-set ranges take precedence.
