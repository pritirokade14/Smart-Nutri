**Subject:** Request for written permission to use IFCT 2017 data in a nutrition app (SmartNutri)

Dear Sir or Madam,

I write on behalf of «FILL: organisation name and registration details», the maker of SmartNutri, a nutrition app for Indian families available on Android and the web. The app helps people log what they eat in familiar Indian household units and see the nutrients in it. It is information only and does not give medical advice.

We would like to use the **Indian Food Composition Tables 2017 (IFCT 2017)**, published by ICMR-National Institute of Nutrition, as the main food database for the app, and we ask for your written permission, since the app is a commercial product («FILL: free with paid plan / free with ads / paid, as applicable»).

**What we would use**
- Food names, food codes, and per-100 g values for energy, protein, carbohydrate, fat, dietary fibre, sugars, sodium and the other nutrients the tables list, for «FILL: all foods, or a named list of about N foods».
- Edible-portion information where given.
- We would not alter the published values. Where we convert to household units (katori, roti and so on) we do it in our own tables and mark the unit grams as ours.

**How we would use it**
- Stored in our database and shown inside the app when a user searches for a food or logs a meal; users see calculated totals for the portion they log.
- Not offered as a bulk download or an API for others.
- Credit shown in the app and on our website, for example: "Indian Food Composition Tables 2017, ICMR-National Institute of Nutrition, Hyderabad", with the edition and a link, and a note when any value is calculated from IFCT data.
- Product wording will not suggest that NIN or ICMR endorses SmartNutri.

**What we ask**
1. Written permission to use IFCT 2017 data in this way in a commercial app, or the terms and any fee that apply.
2. The preferred wording for the credit.
3. Whether a machine-readable copy is available from NIN, and under what terms.
4. Whether we should renew the permission for later editions.

Until we hear from you we will not use IFCT data. In the meantime the app uses USDA FoodData Central (public domain) and clearly labelled recipes.

Thank you for your time. I am glad to provide more detail, a demo or our privacy policy («FILL: link»).
